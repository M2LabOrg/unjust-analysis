# 09 -- New Insights Ideas

## The Gap

The existing report largely confirms what the Home Office already publishes: Black individuals are disproportionately stopped. The user's stated regret is not finding insights beyond what's already known.

Here are angles that go further.

## Insight 1: The Trend Question

**"Is it getting better or worse?"**

The government publishes point-in-time snapshots. By combining 5 years of data (2020/21 -- 2024/25), we can show the *trajectory*. This is not published anywhere in a single view.

- Plot the Black:White IRR year by year
- Statistical test: is the trend significant?
- Context: what policies were introduced in this period?
- Headline: "Despite [X policy], the disparity ratio has [increased/decreased/plateaued] from [A] to [B] over five years"

## Insight 2: The Futility Rate

**"How often do stops on Black individuals find nothing?"**

If outcome data includes whether the searched-for item was found:

- Calculate the "futility rate" = (stops with no find) / (total stops) by ethnicity
- A higher futility rate for one group implies a lower threshold for suspicion
- This directly addresses the question of whether stops are evidence-based or profiling-based
- Headline: "X% of drug searches on Black individuals found no drugs, compared to Y% for White individuals"

## Insight 3: The Regional Outliers

**"Which police forces are the worst -- and which have improved?"**

- Rank all 43 forces by disparity ratio, adjusted for local demographics
- Show which forces are outliers (significantly above/below average)
- Show change over time: "Force X reduced its disparity by Z% while Force Y increased"
- This is actionable: it names specific forces that need scrutiny

## Insight 4: The Age-Ethnicity Intersection

**"Young Black men face a compounding disadvantage"**

- Calculate the combined effect: being young AND Black AND male
- Show this as a multiplicative risk compared to the baseline (older White female)
- Visualize as a "risk multiplier pyramid"
- This puts a number on the lived experience of the most affected group

## Insight 5: Drug Stops vs. Weapons Stops

**"Drug searches are the primary driver of racial disparity"**

- Separate the disparity ratio by reason for search
- Hypothesis (from existing analysis): drug-related stops are more racially skewed than weapons stops
- If confirmed, this focuses policy recommendations on drug-search practices specifically
- Compare across regions

## Insight 6: The "Missing Data" Problem

**"Who is invisible in the statistics?"**

- 18-20% of stops have missing self-defined ethnicity
- Analyse whether missingness is random or systematic
- Which forces have the highest missing rates?
- Is there a correlation between high missing rates and high disparity?
- This is a data governance issue that deserves attention

## Insight 7: Time-of-Day or Seasonal Patterns (if data allows)

- If the open data includes temporal granularity
- Are disparities worse at night? During summer? During specific events?

## Insight 8: The "No Further Action" Cost

**"Quantifying the impact of unnecessary stops"**

- Estimate total person-hours spent on stops that led to NFA, by ethnicity
- Frame it as: "Black communities collectively experienced an estimated X thousand unnecessary police interactions in 2024/25"
- This translates statistics into human impact
